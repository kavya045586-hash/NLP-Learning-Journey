# NLP Vectorization & Model Comparison 📘

This repository documents my learning notes and experiments with **text vectorization techniques** (Bag of Words vs TF‑IDF) and their impact on different machine learning models (Naive Bayes, Logistic Regression).

---

## 📖 Example Sentences
Suppose we have two short documents:

- **Doc1:** "I love playing football"  
- **Doc2:** "Football is love"  

---

### 🔠 Bag of Words (BoW)
BoW just counts word occurrences.

| Word      | Doc1 | Doc2 |
|-----------|------|------|
| I         | 1    | 0    |
| love      | 1    | 1    |
| playing   | 1    | 0    |
| football  | 1    | 1    |
| is        | 0    | 1    |

👉 Representation:  
- Doc1 → `[1,1,1,1,0]`  
- Doc2 → `[0,1,0,1,1]`  

Here, **“love”** and **“football”** are treated the same as **“is”** — all words count equally.

---

### 📊 TF‑IDF
TF‑IDF adjusts weights based on importance.

- Common words across many documents (like “is”) → **low weight**  
- Rare or distinctive words (like “playing”) → **higher weight**  
- Words appearing in both docs (like “love”, “football”) → **moderate weight**

👉 Representation (simplified):  
- Doc1 → `[0.7,0.5,0.9,0.5,0.0]`  
- Doc2 → `[0.0,0.5,0.0,0.5,0.1]`  

---

## ⚖️ Why Accuracy Differs
- **Naive Bayes + BoW** → Works well because NB assumes word independence and raw counts fit its math.  
- **Naive Bayes + TF‑IDF** → Accuracy drops because NB expects integer‑like frequencies, not weighted values.  
- **Logistic Regression + TF‑IDF** → Accuracy improves because LR can exploit weighted features to separate classes better.  

---

## ✅ Intuition
- **BoW** → “Every word is equally important, just count them.”  
- **TF‑IDF** → “Not all words matter equally — rare words tell us more than common ones.”  

---

## 📊 Experimental Results
From my experiments:

- **BoW + Naive Bayes** → ~76% accuracy  
- **TF‑IDF + Naive Bayes** → ~66% accuracy  
- **TF‑IDF + Logistic Regression** → ~86% accuracy
